::manifesto-intro
---
headline: The manifesto
title: Understanding the Cascade model
paragraphs:
  - "This text is a gateway to understanding the Cascade model. Its purpose is not to describe the platform's precise features, but to provide the keys to understanding the Cascade system through three notions: contribution, redistribution, and cycles."
  - "Why should this system matter to you? Because it opens a concrete path toward other forms of economic development, freed from capitalist logics: Cascade enables organizations such as community hubs, cooperatives, associations, or grassroots collectives to collect and manage the contributions that sustain their activities, on a non-profit basis."
  - "When the goal is no longer to generate profit but to allow an activity to exist and endure, an immense field of new possibilities opens up. By imposing logics of competition, value capture, and rent-seeking, the pursuit of profit stifles creativity and fractures the collectives that would rather operate differently. A contributive model like Cascade, by contrast, makes possible a form of development more coherent with the social, political, or ecological aims that these communities carry."
  - "To build this system, we start from existing practices observed in the field, among actors who are already experimenting with other ways of producing and developing services. Cascade offers a generic formalization of these practices, one capable of forming a coherent system."
usagesLabel: "This text can serve:"
usages:
  - "as a guide to understanding the Cascade project."
  - "as a resource for those who wish to get involved in and contribute to Cascade,"
  - "as a reference for rethinking the economy from a post-capitalist perspective."

::manifesto-concepts
---
headline: Vocabulary
title: "Contribution, redistribution, cycles"
description: "The term \"Cascade\" refers at once to the platform, the system, and the basic unit of fundraising. A cascade is a contribution fund geared toward a defined objective, connected to other cascades within a shared, mutualized system. In this model, the aim is not to accumulate the largest possible amount, but to secure activities over time by adapting contributions to real needs. Each cascade then becomes an active building block of a mutualized economic system — interoperable, traceable, and durable. Contribution and distribution are the two key moments in this system, recurring in temporal cycles."
concepts:
  - icon: i-lucide-hand-coins

    description: "The system's basic unit: the full set of means, monetary or otherwise, dedicated to supporting a shared objective."
  - icon: i-lucide-scale

  - icon: i-lucide-repeat

::manifesto-contribution
---
headline: "01 — Contribution"
title: "Contribution, the system's basic unit"
intro:
  - "Cascade is a platform for collecting and managing funds. But unlike its competitors, contribution within Cascade is not a simple fixed price."
  - "By contribution, we mean here the full set of means (monetary or not) directed at supporting a shared objective defined within a cascade. It is neither a donation, nor a price, nor a tax. Its purpose is to help achieve a collective objective."
  - "The shared objective is at the heart of Cascade's contribution mechanism. It represents the amount that needs to be collected during a given cycle to allow a service to exist or a project to come to fruition. Contribution is the means of sharing this effort with the other members of a cascade."
  - "Contribution within Cascade has 5 attributes: it is (1) adaptive, (2) sourced at origin, (3) plural, (4) adjustable, and (5) deferrable."
attributes:
  - title: Contribution is adaptive
    paragraphs:
      - "Cascade contribution adjusts to the collective objective, unlike conventional models that operate by accumulation, where the objective is defined privately and built into a fixed price in order to generate profit. In Cascade, there is always a defined objective to which contributions adjust. For example, if the objective for the base share is 100 € to produce an item or fund a service, and 10 people take part, each can contribute 10 € toward that share. If an eleventh person joins the cascade and the allocation is made automatically per head, each contributor's share can be adjusted down to 9 €. In a conventional system, the price would have stayed at 10 €, generating a surplus captured by the operator."
      - "This attribute of the base share becomes all the more powerful as the number of contributors grows, when its allocation is automatic. The contribution's other shares follow a different logic — adjusted to actual usage rather than to the number of contributors — but carry the same philosophy: aligning the effort asked for with real need rather than with a profit target. This makes possible a kind of shared economic interest between the one offering the service and the one benefiting from it. It is the philosophical foundation of the model: thinking of an economy as a shared effort rather than a mere transaction between producer and consumer."
  - title: Contribution is sourced at origin
    paragraphs:
      - "Contribution directly finances the different components of a service. For example, for a guesthouse, lodging is a service provided separately from the booking service supplied by a platform. Unlike conventional economic models, where the price hides its composition under the guise of simplicity (often to better absorb profit), Cascade contribution has a \"composite price\" structure made up of 4 separate shares:"
    list:
      - "the base share, which corresponds to the objective carried by the cascade, whose amount stays the same regardless of the number of contributors."
      - "the activity share, which corresponds to costs that rise in proportion to the number of users or their level of activity."
      - "the network activity share, which corresponds to the activity share of the other cascades to which it is connected as a contributor."
      - "the over-contribution share, which corresponds to a surplus added voluntarily, arising mechanically, or through forgoing a discount (\"keep the change\")."
    paragraphsAfterList:
      - "Each share is allocated to whatever it finances, which allows for a high degree of transparency in the flow of funds. The network activity share is particularly interesting, because when one of the service's components is itself structured as a cascade, a user's contribution can directly feed several levels of organization at once. This is, notably, how the Cascade platform itself is financed."
      - "This principle extends all the way to collection: when a cost is directly tied to an external service provided by another cascade (a digital tool, a federation, a network partner), each contributor can pay it directly at its source rather than routing it through an intermediary fund that could hide or inflate the amount. In exchange for this direct payment, they can gain access to the governance of the cascade they are paying into."
      - "This turns the service into a solidarity commons, by construction. The structure of the system creates interdependence, and therefore collective resilience."
  - title: Contribution is plural
    paragraphs:
      - "Contribution can be monetary or non-monetary (time, skills, materials, and so on). To move beyond the capitalist paradigm that turns every relationship into a commodity, Cascade seeks to reconcile the flexibility of money with the ability to run services through means other than monetary transactions. The goal is not to rank forms of contribution against one another, but to allow them to coexist on genuinely equal footing. This makes it possible, in particular, to reintegrate and give due credit to volunteer work, which is frequently relied upon in associative and nonprofit models without being recognized as a full component of the value produced. In the absence of this recognition, value capture often occurs once the service becomes sustainable, benefiting those who are later skilled at positioning themselves advantageously. Cascade prevents these structural imbalances through traceability and the fair valuation of all contributions, whatever form they take."
  - title: Contribution is adjustable
    paragraphs:
      - "It can be adjusted after the fact when the objective is hard to define upfront. This mechanism is especially useful when certain components of a service carry a high degree of uncertainty, making it difficult to set an objective in advance. For instance, in a temporary-occupancy setting, costs linked to water, electricity, or gas consumption can fluctuate significantly depending on usage. Likewise, a restaurant operating on the cascade model would not know in advance how many covers a given service will need, which would make it impossible to set an objective ahead of time. In such cases, contribution is provisioned, then adjusted after the fact — upward or downward — based on the reality observed. This allows for greater budgetary precision while preserving the logic of adapting to the real objective."
  - title: Contribution is deferrable
    paragraphs:
      - "It can be committed now and paid later. This mechanism makes room for contributors who, at a given moment, lack immediate resources but still wish to signal their commitment. The most common case is that of an organization being created or in transition, which can declare an intended contribution upfront (for instance through a pledge or a moral agreement) and follow through once its situation allows. Deferred contribution also makes it possible to anticipate a service's gradual ramp-up, giving contributors time to gauge its real price. It thereby strengthens collective sustainability, without excluding those who are out of step, timing-wise, with the financial dynamic."
conclusion:
  - "Cascade thus proposes a contribution logic that is variable in its cost, its composition, and its nature — in contrast to most conventional systems of fundraising or pricing."
  - "This flexibility allows Cascade to remain fair within the collective dynamic, while offering a precise tool for economic engineering. It fits within a logic of dynamic regulation that naturally paves the way for the redistribution discussed in the next section."

::manifesto-redistribution
---
headline: "02 — Redistribution"
title: "Redistribution, the system's internal regulator"
intro:
  - "The attributes of contribution aim to limit, upstream, the creation of surplus or deficit, by adjusting inputs to a collectively defined objective. However, this does not necessarily guarantee an immediate balance between the contributions received and the objective to be reached. Such a balance is achievable when the number of contributors and the target amount are compatible with the group's real capacities, in proportions such that the adjustment remains acceptable to everyone. In practice, imbalances frequently arise:"
imbalances:
  - "either a deficit, when the objective is not reached because the effort required exceeds the collective's real capacities,"
  - "or a surplus, when contributions exceed what was actually needed and the adjustment mechanism was unable to contain it."
surplusOriginsLabel: "While it is easy to understand how a cascade can run a deficit (a shortage of resources and contributors), a surplus can result from more subtle dynamics. A surplus can be:"
surplusOrigins:
  - "voluntary, when a contributor chooses to give more (an over-contribution),"
  - "induced, as a mechanical residue left after contributions are adjusted — this can be substantial when a service has a very low marginal cost and a large number of contributors, as can be the case for a mature digital tool,"
  - "reactive, for example when a contributor whose contribution is adjusted downward decides to leave the difference (\"keep the change\")."
cycleEffectsIntro: "Because Cascade operates in cycles, these imbalances are not fixed over time:"
cycleEffects:
  - "a deficit can accumulate and form a collective debt that contributors choose to pay down over successive cycles,"
  - "a surplus must be put to use: it can help pay down a debt, support another cascade, temporarily lower contributions, or be set aside in reserve for future use."
conclusion:
  - "Acting on surplus or deficit is what makes it possible to properly regulate a cascade. While its execution can be automated through rules defined in advance, choosing those rules remains a collective decision-making process."

::manifesto-cycles
---
headline: "03 — Cycles"
title: "Cycles, the system's temporal rhythm"
intro:
  - "Contributions and regulation take place over time according to cycles: defined periods (for example a month, a quarter, or six months) during which a cascade collects contributions and activates its internal mechanisms (adaptation to the objective, redistribution, regularization, governance, and so on)."
  - "Cycles are essential because they give rhythm to the economic life of a project or collective: they make it possible to structure key moments, align development ambitions, adapt contributions to the pace of needs (slow or fast), and schedule regular moments of regulation."
  - "They embody a kind of breathing rhythm within Cascade's economic model, framing the circulation of contributions over time while opening windows for democratic deliberation on matters that are concrete, measurable, and specific. There are two types:"
types:
  - icon: i-lucide-flag
    title: Finite cycles
    description: "For projects with a deadline (e.g. a campaign, an event, a one-off development)."
  - icon: i-lucide-infinity
    title: Continuous cycles
    description: "For recurring activities or services (e.g. a platform, a community hub, a cooperative, and so on)."
conclusion:
  - "Cycles also define an essential characteristic of contribution: its frequency. A contributor can take part in every cycle and thereby operate within a recurring logic, or contribute only once, on a one-off basis. This distinction matters because it shapes how we think about contributors' legitimacy to take part in a cascade's governance."
  - "A contributor active over several cycles can be seen as more directly invested in the project's dynamic, while a one-off contributor may play a more limited role. To avoid free-rider effects or opportunistic capture of decision-making processes, threshold mechanisms can be put in place. These make it possible, for example, to reserve certain decisions for contributors who have taken part in a minimum number of cycles, or who have sustained a certain level of contribution over time."
  - "This approach makes it possible to link contribution frequency to a recognized degree of involvement, without excluding those who can only contribute occasionally. It thereby contributes to governance that is fairer and more context-sensitive, giving proper weight to sustained, real engagement over time."

headline: Where Cascade stands today
title: "From today's tool to the Cascade vision"
description: "Today's Cascade is not a watered-down version of this vision: it is its foundation. Subscription models, typed contributions, and the cascade structure already lay down the concrete architecture on which the mechanisms described above will be built."
todayLabel: "Today"

steps:
  - today: "A subscription model sets a configured amount once and for all."
    tomorrow: "Each contributor's share of a shared objective adjusts automatically to the number of active contributors — the same mechanic, applied continuously."
  - today: "A contribution is typed as Fixed, Adaptive, or One-off, but remains a single amount per line."
    tomorrow: "Each contribution breaks down into 4 explicit shares — base, activity, network activity, over-contribution — for full transparency of flows."
  - today: "Payments are tracked by method (direct debit, bank transfer, card)."
    tomorrow: "Contribution becomes plural: time, skills, or materials are recognized on the same footing as money."
  - today: "Invoicing is issued and sent automatically when due."
    tomorrow: "Contribution becomes deferrable and adjustable: committed now, adjusted or paid later according to observed reality."
  - today: "Management dashboards provide indicators per cascade: occupancy, contributions, pending invoices."
    tomorrow: "Redistribution becomes an active mechanism: surpluses and deficits are put to collective use, cycle after cycle."
  - today: "A cascade manages a place or a project, with its own contributors and its own objective."
    tomorrow: "Cascades connect into a network: the network activity share directly finances several levels of organization, including the federation itself."

::manifesto-use-cases
---
headline: Use cases
title: "The Cascade logic applied to very different contexts"
description: "Each case helps to better understand how the logics of contribution, redistribution, and cycles apply in practice, while highlighting the concrete advantages over a conventional economic system."
cases:
  - icon: i-lucide-map-pin-house
    title: Community hub
    paragraphs:
      - "An association manages a building shared by several occupants: craftspeople, trainers, cultural groups. Each has different usage patterns, rhythms, and financial capacities. With Cascade, a shared fund automatically adjusts occupants' contributions based on their actual usage and the costs to be covered (heating, electricity, coordination, and so on)."
      - "At the end of each cycle, surpluses are reallocated according to collectively defined rules: a temporary reduction in rent, investment in the shared space, or support for another organization in the same network. Once a year, a collectively defined equalization mechanism helps balance the budgets among the various organizations involved in running the community hub."
    advantageLabel: Model advantage
    advantage: "temporary occupancy often comes with uncertainty around costs and resources. Cascade makes it possible to allocate contributions fairly based on actual usage, act on imbalances over time, and turn management into a traceable, cooperative process."
  - icon: i-lucide-share-2
    title: Cooperative platform
    paragraphs:
      - "A small team maintains software used by around fifty local bike-delivery cooperatives joined together in a cooperative federation. To guarantee the platform's maintenance and development, a continuous cascade is set up. It covers hosting, support, and development work, while adapting each cooperative's contribution to its means. In addition, the grants the federation occasionally receives help lower contributions."
      - "Smaller cooperatives can contribute on a deferred basis and/or in kind (presenting the project to newcomers, writing documentation, testing). Surpluses are earmarked for a fund that helps guarantee the federation's stability. Over time, the platform reached sufficient technical maturity: development needs decreased, and costs became concentrated on maintenance and support. Rather than lowering the objective, the federation chose to maintain the contribution level in order to fund shared positions, particularly for business development serving the whole network."
      - "This use case is also the archetypal example of the Cascade platform's own economic model."
    advantageLabel: Model advantage
    advantage: "unlike a model based on service fees or venture capital, Cascade enables distributed structural viability, driven by collective needs and contributive cycles."
  - icon: i-lucide-utensils
    title: Neighborhood pay-what-you-can canteen
    paragraphs:
      - "A network of canteens and restaurants uses Cascade as its checkout payment system. The model is built around a single meal price, deliberately set above the direct cost price. This price covers not only food costs but also guarantees fair pay for staff and stabilizes the activity."
      - "Every meal paid for feeds a daily cascade. At the end of service, customers are offered a form of regulation: a discount on their next meal if actual costs came in under the objective. Some customers, particularly those who are better off or attuned to the approach, choose to \"leave the change\" and don't ask for that discount. These surpluses, aggregated over the month, are transferred to a cascade shared among the canteens."
      - "This inter-canteen fund makes it possible to finance free events in working-class neighborhoods: solidarity meals, cultural activities, public debates. The Cascade model thus allows a local economy to organize at a national scale, secure its own viability, and carry out social projects aligned with the collective's values."
    advantageLabel: Model advantage
    advantage: "instead of unpredictable pay-what-you-can pricing, a stable yet adjustable price guarantees the canteens' viability, while channeling surpluses back into collective action at a national scale."
  - icon: i-lucide-party-popper
    title: Cultural festival
    paragraphs:
      - "A collective organizes a cultural festival. The event lasts three days, but the organizing work spans several months. The cascade is therefore configured as a finite cycle divided into several phases: presale, regular sale, and the event itself. Each phase has its own objective, matching that phase's specific needs."
      - "The ticket price is initially set at 25 €, but because contribution is adaptive, this price falls as the number of festival-goers grows: the more people commit, the closer the price gets to the real cost per participant. At the end of each phase, an adjustment offers early buyers a discount (or credit) if the objective was exceeded."
      - "The surplus generated is reallocated to free activities during the festival (workshops, open stage) and to a support fund for the following year's edition."
    advantageLabel: Model advantage
    advantage: "Cascade makes it possible to handle time-bound activities like events, with an entry cost that falls thanks to the effect of scale."
  - icon: i-lucide-shopping-cart
    title: Cooperative supermarket
    paragraphs:
      - "A cooperative supermarket relies on bulk purchasing, a low or nonexistent margin, and active cooperation among suppliers, members, and managers. Its main challenge: reaching economic balance while guaranteeing full transparency over financial flows. With Cascade, the model is strengthened through an integrated, regular contributive system."
      - "In the early cycles, product prices can be adjusted to test the break-even point: set too low, it endangers viability; set too high, it compromises accessibility. Cascade makes it possible to calibrate this threshold dynamically, cycle after cycle. Once balance is reached and the cooperative has settled into a steady rhythm, prices can be lowered thanks to new contributor-customers joining and to economies of scale, all while maintaining financial stability."
      - "A portion of the price paid by members feeds a regulation fund. This fund can absorb unsold products, support struggling producers, or finance investment in shared infrastructure (cold storage, logistics equipment, and so on). At the end of the month, a regulation cycle redistributes the surpluses: vouchers for members, a one-off price reduction, or support for local initiatives."
    advantageLabel: Model advantage
    advantage: "Cascade not only makes internal economic flows visible, but also lets the supermarket's balance be adjusted cycle after cycle. It offers a concrete answer to the cooperative promise: accessibility, stability, solidarity, and local roots."
  - icon: i-lucide-music
    title: Music meta-label
    paragraphs:
      - "A network of traditional musicians from villages and small towns across France regularly plays at community dances and local festivities. At the end of each event, they sell their self-produced CDs, often on a very local scale."
      - "To overcome their individual isolation, they decide to create a contributive meta-label using Cascade. A single national cascade is set up to pool the costs of physically producing the CDs (pressing, printing, booklet), graphic design, shipping logistics, and also online distribution through a shared platform."
      - "Each musician or small group contributes to the cascade in proportion to the number of albums they wish to produce or sell, but also according to their means: some give more to support the collective, others contribute in kind (graphic design, distribution, joint tours). Sales made locally are reported at the end of each cycle (every quarter), which triggers a regularization: contributions are adjusted, surpluses are redistributed, and funds are reinvested in new projects. It works like a reversed distribution network: each person sells within their own territory, but the effort of production is borne collectively."
    advantageLabel: Model advantage
    advantage: "by eliminating the intermediary costs tied to small print runs or commercial subcontracting, musicians gain in margin, autonomy, and quality, all while maintaining an independent, craft-based approach."

::manifesto-objections
---
headline: Common objections
title: "What we often hear in response"
objections:
  - quote: "Cascade doesn't guarantee a project's economic viability"
    paragraphs:
      - "\"...OK, so you're organizing a more collective and transparent approach. But if nobody wants to contribute, or if the objective is unrealistic, the system fails anyway.\""
      - "This limitation is real, and Cascade is no magic trick — it is a mechanism for dynamic balance between needs and contributions. The platform does not guarantee that a project will succeed, and it replaces neither collective commitment nor the need for a realistic objective. What it does do is make the effort required to carry out an activity adjustable and transparent. Where other models hide costs or isolate contributors, Cascade makes it possible to establish a shared objective, surface any gaps, and regulate them. Cascade can also help set initiatives in motion by making tangible the benefit of scaling up contribution to a service, through the resulting downward trend in its access cost. It is this capacity to activate cooperation that sets Cascade apart from a simple payment tool or a conventional fundraising campaign."
  - quote: "Price adaptation already happens under capitalism"
    paragraphs:
      - "\"...What you call adaptive contribution is already what a tiered discount or a loyalty discount does.\""
      - "It's worth stressing the fundamental difference: here, adaptation happens against a known shared objective, defined collectively. In a capitalist model, price flexibility is aimed above all at capturing as much value as possible. In Cascade, adaptation serves to align the contribution with a real, identified, transparent need. It is the difference between a price that fluctuates to extract value, and a contribution that adjusts to meet a shared objective. This transforms the underlying economic logic: you are not paying \"whatever price you're willing to pay,\" but what is actually necessary for the service to work for everyone."
  - quote: "There isn't enough information about how it actually works"
    paragraphs:
      - "\"...How is the objective set? Is it an arbitrary amount? An accounting calculation? How does regulation happen? Is it automatic, semi-automatic, collectively approved? Who governs a cascade? The lead organizer? A collective? The contributors?\""
      - "This text does not aim to detail every technical mechanism or parameter of the system, but to convey a structural logic flexible enough to apply to a wide diversity of contexts. One of Cascade's major strengths is its ability to be configured according to needs on the ground: some cascades can be run collectively, others delegated; some objectives can be set from projected accounting figures, others through a political process."
      - "Cascade does not impose a single model but offers a framework that is both coherent and evolving. This flexibility is made possible by the fact that the system is designed as a digital commons, continuously improved by a team mandated by its users to strengthen its genericity, its robustness, and its capacity to give rise to new uses."
  - quote: "This is great, I love the idea! But isn't it too complicated to understand or explain?"
    paragraphs:
      - "\"...We need something simple. If we have to explain the cascade, adjustable contribution, cycles... it's going to be too much for our users.\""
      - "Cascade is also a tool for collective learning. It is designed to allow for a gradual rise in fluency, with simple interfaces. It is also possible to configure very simple use cases, with few visible elements, and then progressively enrich the model as the collective matures. It's a modular tool, not a dogma."
      - "Above all, Cascade is being built incrementally: we are only at the very beginning. The system presented here lays out a philosophy and a basic architecture, but its development is still ongoing. This means that this philosophy can be adapted as new, concrete needs emerge. It is real-world usage, contexts, and people on the ground who will guide its evolution, so that it remains relevant, accessible, and workable across a wide range of situations."
  - quote: "Won't this discourage institutional funders?"
    paragraphs:
      - "\"If everything is subject to open-ended regulation, how do you present a clear budget to the regional authority?\""
      - "Cascade does not replace accounting. It makes it easier to justify and to read, by aligning contributions with quantified objectives. Funders can themselves contribute, or observe how a cascade operates in full transparency. This can even strengthen their confidence in the sound management of resources."
  - quote: "Why not simply rely on sponsorship or crowdfunding?"
    paragraphs:
      - "Crowdfunding is a one-off, often emotionally driven act, whereas Cascade organizes a lasting contributive economy. It is not a call for support — it is an operating model in which everyone knows why they're giving, what it is used for, and can take part in the collective adjustment."
  - quote: "How can we be sure that the use of contributions is genuinely non-profit?"
    paragraphs:
      - "\"...Nothing stops someone from diverting the system for their own benefit, or claiming to follow a contributive logic while actually capturing value.\""
      - "Cascade relies on structural and legal mechanisms that govern the use of contributions to guarantee their non-profit nature:"
    list:
      - "A shared governance framework: cascades can be integrated into a federation, or subject to rules of transparency and mutual oversight. Decisions about how funds are used can be made visible, open to review, and voted on by contributors."
      - "A traceable technical system: each share of a contribution is earmarked for an identified use (fixed cost, activity, network, surplus), which makes any capture of value visible."
      - "A coherent legal framework: Cascade relies on free-license models and collective ownership of its tools, which prevent their later privatization. The platform itself is a digital commons."
    paragraphsAfterList:
      - "This is not a matter of trusting people's intentions, but of designing a system that structurally prevents private capture. The model is built to make profit-seeking technically and politically undesirable."
